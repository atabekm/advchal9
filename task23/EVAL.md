# Reranking, filtering and query rewriting

Fifteen control questions about the three indexed documents ([questions.json](questions.json)):
the ten from task 22, plus two answerable questions worded vaguely on purpose (q11, q12) and
three near-miss unanswerable ones (q13–q15: on-topic, but the answer is not in the document).
Every question goes through each retrieval mode, all over the `struct` chunks:

| column | stage 0: rewrite | stage 1: search | stage 2: rerank / filter |
|---|---|---|---|
| **base** | — | cosine top 5 | — (task 22 rag/struct) |
| **cos-filter** | — | cosine top 5 | drop chunks more than 0.06 below the best cosine score |
| **rerank@0** | — | cosine top 20 | cross-encoder, top 5, no threshold |
| **rerank** | — | cosine top 20 | cross-encoder, top 5, score ≥ 0.05 |
| **rerank@0.3** | — | cosine top 20 | cross-encoder, top 5, score ≥ 0.3 |
| **rewrite** | question + 1–3 rewrites | top 20 per query, fused by reciprocal rank | — (fused top 5) |
| **rewrite+rerank@0** | question + 1–3 rewrites | top 20 per query, fused | cross-encoder (best score over the queries), top 5 |
| **rewrite+rerank** | question + 1–3 rewrites | top 20 per query, fused | the same, score ≥ 0.3 |

The thresholds come from `rag calibrate` (the strictest cutoff that loses no hit; see
[Calibration](#calibration)). `@0` columns are reranking without filtering, and `rerank@0.3` is
the stricter neighbour, so the table separates what reordering does from what cutting does.

Scoring is the same as in task 22: a **keyword check**, an **LLM judge** (correct / partial /
wrong / refused + hallucination flag, never sees the mode or the context), and **retrieval**
checks (an expected source in the candidate pool, and in the context that reaches the LLM).
When a filter leaves no chunk, the answer is "The documents do not contain the answer." without
an LLM call; those are counted separately. Raw answers and verdicts are in [eval/](eval/); the
block between the markers is generated by `uv run rag eval --modes … --markdown EVAL.md`.

## Reading

**Answer quality barely moves, and most of the movement is noise.** Judge scores sit between
11.0 and 12.5 out of 15. The two rewrite+rerank columns are on top (12.5 and 12.0, against 11.5
for base), with the best keyword scores (92%) and the only 10 / 10 retrieval. But one run with
the same model answering and judging is not precise to ±1.5 points. On the 8 questions where
`base` and `rewrite` sent the LLM the same set of chunks (in a different order), the verdicts
still differ 3 times (q02, q03, q06), and q01 gets "partial" in two columns whose answers state
the same facts as the "correct" ones. Only the differences that show up in the retrieval
columns and in the answers themselves are worth reading.

**Reranking reorders correctly.** On q04, q06 and q07 the expected chunk moves from cosine
rank 2–3 to rank 1. q06 is the clearest case: base explains Rule One and says Rules Two and
Three are missing (the judge calls it wrong). Every rerank column puts the chunk with all three
rules first (cosine rank 6 in the pool of 20) and answers "Calories, Protein, Consistency".

**Only rewrite + rerank fixes the multi-document miss (q08).** In every other column the
TatarTTS paper takes all five slots. Rewriting alone adds an "Apertium Tatar machine
translation" query, but reciprocal rank fusion still ranks the TatarTTS chunks first, and the
rewrite answer says no other document mentions Tatar (judged wrong). Reranking the fused pool
against the question alone also fails: the cross-encoder scores the Apertium chunks ≤ 0.007 for
"Which documents deal with the Tatar language", but up to 0.999 for the rewritten query. So
rewrite+rerank scores each chunk by its best score over the question and its rewrites. With
that, two Apertium chunks (the released Tatar and Kazakh–Tatar pairs) reach the top 5, and the
answer names both documents.

**The threshold filter saves tokens, not answers.** At the calibrated cutoffs, rerank keeps
4.3 chunks on average and rewrite+rerank keeps 3.9, cutting prompt tokens by 10–20% with no
answerable question emptied. The quality change is within noise (rerank 11.5 vs rerank@0
11.5; rewrite+rerank 12.0 vs 12.5 at @0, where the difference is q06 going from correct to
partial with the same three rules in both answers). A stricter cutoff hurts: rerank@0.3
empties the contexts of q08 and q12, so two answerable questions are refused without asking
the LLM, and the score drops to 10.0.

**A threshold can't recognise near-miss questions.** All five unanswerable questions are
refused in every column; the "answer only from the context" prompt already does that. The
filter only catches the off-topic one (q09, creatine, best score 0.225). The near misses
score as high as real answers: q13 ("how many native speakers rated TatarTTS") gets 0.956 from
the abstract, which mentions the MOS evaluation and doesn't contain the number. A
cross-encoder measures topical relevance; "does this passage contain the answer" is a question
for the generator.

**Rewriting alone changes little.** `rewrite` scores 11.0 against 11.5 for base, but for 8 of
the 15 questions its top 5 is the same set of chunks as base, so most of that difference is
answer and judge variance (q03 refuses with the chunks base answered from). Where the set does
change, fusion rarely gets a new chunk past the ones the question already found: the rewrites
are paraphrases of the question, so they find mostly the same chunks, and RRF ranks chunks
found by several queries first. The rewrite pays off only together with the reranker, which
sorts the larger pool (q08).

**Where stage 2 made things worse:**

- *q11 (vague "how was that Turkic voice dataset put together")*: the cross-encoder prefers
  the abstract and the related-work paragraph (both "about" building a dataset) over §III.A,
  which actually describes the corpus, the actors and the studio. The three plain rerank
  columns answer from the corpus statistics and are judged wrong; base, rewrite and
  rewrite+rerank (which also searches "TatarTTS dataset construction") are partial. The
  retrieval column still says "hit" because §III.A shares page 2 with the chunks that were
  kept. Page-level ground truth is too coarse, as task 22 found for q04.
- *q12 (vague "cheap drink trick … when exactly", age 27)*: the answer depends on the caveat on
  p. 34 (in your 20s, drink immediately before the meal). Reranked against this wording, the
  preload chunks score only 0.07–0.4, the p. 34 chunk is cut, and the rerank answer gives the
  30-minute timing from the studies without the age caveat (judged wrong). No column gets this
  fully right.
- *q02 ("which architecture and training framework")*: §IV.A, the paragraph that names Piper,
  is cosine #5 and makes the base context. The reranker prefers the conclusion, the abstract and
  the related work, so §IV.A drops out, and the rerank answers say no framework is named.

**Cost.** The reranker adds 0.7 s for 20 chunks on an M1 (MPS) and 1.9 s for a fused pool of
~35 chunks × 2–4 queries. The rewrite adds 3.35 s and ~830 tokens per question: `deepseek-flash`
spends ~570 completion tokens (mostly reasoning) to write 1–3 short queries. The full pipeline
answers in 6.9 s against 2.2 s for base.

**About the judge.** As in task 22, the hallucination flags are mostly false positives. The
judge counts true facts from the documents as unsupported when they are not in the written
expectation (q11's 70 hours and 38,000 segments, q12's "44% faster weight loss", which is on
p. 33). The keyword column is the independent check, and it agrees with the ranking above.

### What to use

`rewrite+rerank` at 0.3: it is the only mode that puts every expected source in the context,
it refuses the off-topic question without an LLM call, and it sends the fewest chunks (3.9 on
average). Its price is three times the latency of base, almost all of it in the rewrite. When
latency matters more than the multi-document case, plain `rerank` at 0.05 adds 0.7 s to base
and fixes the ordering misses (q04, q06, q07). In both, the threshold is a cost control, set
low enough that no answerable question is emptied, not a relevance oracle.
