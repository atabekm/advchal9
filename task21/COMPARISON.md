# Chunking strategies compared

**Corpus:** 3 PDFs, 95 pages, 193,505 characters:
- TatarTTS: a 5-page IEEE conference paper in two columns, headings found by typography.
- Gutless: a 62-page e-book, headings found by font size.
- Apertium: a 28-page Springer journal article, headings from its outline.

**Strategies:**
- **fixed:** a 1000-character window with 150 overlap, cut at whitespace.
- **struct:** one chunk per section, where sections longer than 1500 characters are packed on paragraph and sentence boundaries, and sections shorter than 200 characters are merged forward.

Both cut the same extracted text, so every difference below comes from where they cut. Every chunk is embedded with `nomic-embed-text` (768 dimensions).

The tables are generated by `uv run indexer stats --markdown COMPARISON.md`. Only the block between the markers is rewritten; the text around it stays.

## What the numbers say

### Boundaries: struct cuts where the author did
Fixed starts mid-sentence in **77.8%** of chunks and ends mid-sentence in
**92.2%**. That's what a window blind to text produces: a cut lands on a
sentence end only by luck, and the overlap does not change that.

Struct starts mid-sentence in **0.6%** of chunks. Every chunk opens on a
heading, a paragraph or a sentence. It still ends "mid-sentence" in
**19.9%**, and almost none of those are real mid-sentence cuts. They are
text that isn't prose:
- reference list entries (`… Comput Linguist`, `… In: Proceedings of the`);
- tables (`Tatar–Bashqortd 70.19 8.97 – –`);
- author and affiliation blocks and URL footnotes;
- a running header the extractor missed.

A handful of real ones remain: a paragraph continued after a page break by a
word that starts with a capital or a digit, which the glue rule (it expects a
lowercase continuation) doesn't catch.

### Topic purity: fixed mixes sections, and more so the shorter they are
**22.6%** of fixed chunks contain text from two or more sections, against
**2.8%** for struct. How often fixed mixes depends on how long a document's
sections are:

| document | average section | fixed chunks mixing sections |
|---|---:|---:|
| TatarTTS: 14 headings on 5 pages | ~1,600 chars | 41% |
| Apertium: 26 headings on 28 pages | ~3,000 chars | 31% |
| Gutless: 14 headings on 62 pages | ~6,600 chars | 11% |

When sections are about the size of the window, almost every window crosses
a boundary. The struct chunks that mix are heading-only sections merged into
their first subsection (`III. DATASET CONSTRUCTION` + `A. Tatar Speech
Corpus`), which is the intended behaviour.

One chunk shows the difference. Fixed `1570978467:fixed:0004` is labelled
`I. INTRODUCTION` and holds:
- the end of the contributions list;
- the paper outline;
- two URL footnotes;
- the headings `II. RELATED WORKS` and `A. NLP for Turkic Languages`;
- the first sentence of that subsection, cut off mid-word-group.

Its vector averages three topics, and its `section` label is wrong for most
of its text. Struct `1570978467:struct:0004` starts at `II. RELATED WORKS >
A. NLP for Turkic Languages` and holds only that subsection.

### Size: fixed is uniform, struct follows the document
Fixed chunks are 991 ± 46 characters; only each document's last chunk is
shorter. Struct chunks are 1,074 ± 398, from 422 (p10) to 1,461 (p90):
- short sections stay short;
- long ones fill up to the 1,500-character limit;
- 2.2% (4 chunks) are under 200 characters: three are left over when a
  section's paragraphs don't pack evenly (Gutless's Q&A chapter of short
  answers), and one is the tail of Springer's licence notice.

Uneven sizes are the price of clean boundaries. Whether retrieval minds them
is a question for the next task.

### Cost: struct is cheaper
The 150-character overlap makes fixed store **1.18×** the corpus, against
**1.01×** for struct (merged headings only). Fixed also makes 27% more
chunks, which means:
- 230 vectors against 181;
- 17% more embedding tokens (54,049 against 46,135);
- 1.2× the embedding time;
- 0.94 MB of storage against 0.75 MB.

At this corpus size all of it is small. It grows linearly with the corpus.

### Redundancy: fixed neighbours say the same thing
Consecutive chunks of a document have a mean cosine similarity of **0.835**
for fixed and **0.788** for struct. Fixed neighbours share 150 characters of
text and often the same section, so a top-5 search is more likely to return
near-duplicates. Struct neighbours are separate sections, so the same five
slots cover more ground.

## Where struct depends on extraction
Struct is only as good as the headings found. This corpus exercised all
three sources of headings:
- **Outline:** Apertium. Its outline nests every section under the paper
  title, so each section path starts with the full title (`Recent advances
  in Apertium, … > 4 New modules > 4.3 Anaphora resolution`). The path is
  correct but long.
- **Font size:** Gutless. Headings are found, but the levels come from font
  size and don't match the book's logic: `Chapter 3` ends up nested under
  `3. MEAL FREQUENCY`, a numbered point from chapter 2. The chunk
  *boundaries* are still right; only the path in `section` is misleading.
- **IEEE typography:** TatarTTS. Before the fix for Roman numeral and italic
  headings, this paper had 0 headings. Struct then fell back to packing
  paragraphs: clean sentence boundaries, but no sections.

When a PDF gives no heading signal at all, struct turns into
paragraph-aware fixed-size chunking. That is still better than a raw
window, but it isn't structure.

## Verdict
**Struct wins on every quality measure and costs less:**
- 0.6% of chunks start mid-sentence, against 77.8%;
- 2.8% mix sections, against 22.6%;
- a correct `section` label on every chunk;
- 1.01× storage, against 1.18×.

**Fixed keeps two advantages:**
- it needs nothing from the document, so it works the same on any PDF;
- it makes chunks of predictable size.

For this index, **struct should be the default** for the retrieval tasks
that follow. Fixed stays as the baseline, to check whether cleaner chunks
actually give better answers.
